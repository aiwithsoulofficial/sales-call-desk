# Bespoke sales call

For Kelly | Done-for-you systems built around the client's business

Read the quoted words. Italic notes are for you. Follow the answers rather than asking every prompt. Brackets are details from this call. Say the quote yourself; never store a price in this script.

*Before the call: read their enquiry and previous conversations. Know what they have already bought or attended. Have their current tools and stated priority in view. Use the agreed call length. A quote is only ready when scope, dependencies, delivery and payment terms are clear.*

## 1. Open with what they want built

> Hi [name], Kelly here from Ai With Soul. You mentioned you wanted help with [their actual enquiry]. What's happening with that at the moment?

*For a workshop attendee, acknowledge the work they have already done:*

> Since the workshop, how have you gone with [the thing they worked on]? What would you like help building next?

*Set the purpose:*

> I'd like to understand the job you want taken off your plate and how it works now. Then I'll tell you what I'd recommend building, what I need to check, and the next step. Sound good?

## 2. Find the one priority

> What's the main thing you want help with first?

> What has made it a priority now?

> If we could make one part of that easier, which part would make the biggest difference to you?

*If they give you a long list:*

> I've noted those down. Which one needs to work first for the others to be useful?

*If they want to explore everything:*

> We can map the rest after we've made the first job clear. Let's start with [their priority].

## 3. Understand the job and its boundaries

> Walk me through the last time you did that. What started it, what did you do, and what happened at the end?

*Use only the follow-ups their answer leaves open:*

> Who else touches it?

> How often does it happen?

> Which systems hold the information, and where should the finished result go?

> Where do things get delayed or need correcting?

> What still needs a person to check or approve?

> Are there confidential details or company permissions we need to account for?

> Who can approve access to those systems?

*You need enough detail to judge the job. Do not design the entire solution on the sales call.*

## 4. Understand the impact and the decision

> What does doing it this way take out of your week?

> What gets pushed back while you or the team deal with it?

> What have you tried already, and what would you want handled differently this time?

> Once it's working, what would you expect to see happen without you having to chase it?

> Who else needs to be involved in agreeing the work?

> Is there a date this needs to be working by? What's happening then?

*Check dates and capacity before committing. Use their own figures if they offer them; do not manufacture an ROI calculation.*

## 5. Say back the proposed result

> Let me check I've understood. You want [specific job] to take [agreed input] and produce [agreed result], with [person] checking [approval point]. At the moment, you're [their current burden]. You'd like to be able to [their desired change]. Is that right?

> We'd know the first build was working when [observable acceptance check]. Does that cover what matters most?

*An acceptance check describes a real end-to-end job, including the handoff and any human approval. Do not substitute “the integration is installed” for a working result.*

## 6. Explain the bespoke service

> I'd recommend starting with [defined first build], because it addresses [their stated priority].

> We do the work online, so location is not a barrier. I scope it with you, build it in your own accounts, test the agreed job from start to finish, then show you and [the people who will use it] how to run it.

> We'll agree what is included, what I need from you and how we'll check it works. Any additional jobs get agreed before we add them.

> There's a one-month minimum for bespoke work. The delivery dates depend on the scope and access we confirm. I'll make those clear in the agreement.

> You keep the system. After the first month, we can agree further building or optional ongoing support. The retainer includes new skills, one strategy session a month, and maintenance and upgrades. We'll agree what support you need and the terms before it starts.

> How does that sound against what you wanted taken off your plate?

*Keep the explanation tied to their job. Do not introduce a workshop or a membership as another thing they must buy.*

## 7. Present the scope and fee, or close on the missing check

### A. You have enough information to quote

> For the agreed scope, I'd build [deliverables in plain words]. We'd check it by [acceptance check]. I would need [specific access or input] from you, and the delivery timing is [confirmed timing]. [Specific excluded work] would be separate.

> The fee for that scope is [say the quote and applicable tax terms]. The payment terms are [state the agreed terms]. [Explain any separate software or usage charges that apply].

*Stop and let them respond. Then:*

> Would you like to go ahead with that scope?

*If yes:*

> Great. Let's go through the agreement so we're clear on the deliverables, dates and payment terms. Once that's agreed, we'll arrange the payment due under those terms and book the first setup session.

*If the agreement is ready, review it with them. Only send the applicable secure payment link under the approved terms. Never take money for an unconfirmed build.*

> Who should be at that first session, and who can organise the access we need?

### B. You need to verify something before quoting

> I can see a useful first build here. I need to check [specific connection, access, requirement or delivery question] before I give you a firm scope and fee.

> My next step is [your specific check]. I need [their input] from you. Can we book [a time you've checked] to go through the recommendation and quote, with [other decision maker] there too?

*Record the owner and date for each missing input. This is a scoping follow-up, not a paid yes. Do not promise the integration works because it sounds possible.*

## When they raise an objection

*Ask what they mean, answer that concern, then ask whether it helped. One clear no ends the sales conversation.*

### “It's more than I expected.”

> What were you expecting it to cover?

*Listen before explaining the scope again.*

> Is the concern the amount available, or whether solving [their priority] is worth that amount to the business?

*Value concern:*

> Let's check the result we've scoped. You said [their priority and impact]. This build covers [relevant deliverable] and we would test it by [acceptance check]. Which part feels uncertain?

*Budget concern:*

> If that amount doesn't work for you, we can pause. If there is a smaller first build that would still solve a useful problem within the one-month minimum, I can scope that separately.

*Only offer a smaller scope if it stands on its own and you are willing to deliver it. Never give the same work at a discount.*

### “I need to think about it.”

> Of course. What are the main things you're weighing up?

*Answer what they name, then:*

> Would you like us to put a time in to talk after you've considered that?

*If they decline, leave it there.*

### “I need my partner or team involved.”

> That makes sense. What will they need to understand or check?

> Let's include them when we go through the scope, so you don't have to explain all of this on your own. What time would suit everyone?

### “I've paid someone before and got nothing useful.”

> What was missing when they handed it over?

*Let them explain.*

> Then let's make [the missing requirement] explicit in this scope. We can write down what you need to see working, how we'll check it, and what gets handed over to you.

*Show the specific proposed acceptance check. Do not promise results from a different client's project.*

### “I don't have time to manage another project.”

> Which part are you worried will fall back on you?

> I'd need your input on [the actual business decisions], access from [the relevant person], and time to check the result together. I handle the build. Let's agree when we need you so you can see what you're committing to.

*Do not promise a hands-off project or a time commitment until scoped.*

### “Can it work with our current systems?”

> Tell me which systems and which information needs to move between them.

*If verified, explain the supported connection. If not:*

> I'll check that connection and any access restrictions before it goes into the agreed scope.

### “Who owns it? What if we stop working together?”

> The system is built in your accounts and handed over to you. Your software subscriptions remain yours to manage. We'll make the handover and any ongoing support clear in the agreement, so you know what you can run and what help you're choosing afterwards.

### “What if it breaks?”

> We'll agree the checks and handover for the build, and set out the support terms. Software can change, so ongoing maintenance is something we discuss. If you choose the retainer, we'll confirm what it covers and how you ask for help.

*Do not promise unlimited access or a response time that is not in the agreement.*

### “What about privacy and sensitive information?”

> Before we connect anything, we'll identify what information is involved, who is allowed to access it and which tools your business permits. Who needs to approve that on your side?

*Keep the scope conditional on that review. Own accounts are not a guarantee of local-only processing, security or compliance.*

### “What's your hourly or day rate?”

> I quote for the agreed work and the result we test, so you can see what you're buying. Are you comparing this with something else?

### “Send me a proposal.”

> Yes. What do you need it to make clear for you?

> I'll cover [their requested points], the scope, timing and terms. Can we put a time in to go through it together, with anyone else who needs to decide?

*Agree a realistic delivery time. No assumption that requesting a proposal means they have bought.*

### “Can we add these other jobs too?”

> I've noted them. Let's decide whether they need to be in the first build or can follow it. I'll confirm any change to scope, timing and fee before we add them.

## If it is a no

> Thanks for talking it through with me. I appreciate you being clear.

*For a genuine fit issue, name the reason. Offer a relevant referral only if appropriate and with their consent.*
