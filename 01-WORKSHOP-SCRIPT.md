# Workshop sales call

For Kelly | One-day, in-person workshop | Allow 30 minutes

Read the quoted words. Italic notes are for you. Ask one question, then listen. Skip anything they have already answered. Brackets are the buyer's details; the price is said by you and never stored here.

*Before the call: read their enquiry, confirm their city and a current workshop date, check the venue and booking link. Have the current fee, payment terms and required software costs ready. Phone first; use the same script on Zoom if already booked. Offer only a confirmed room in their own city unless they ask about another. Do not offer an old date or an unconfirmed room.*

## 1. Open and make the call comfortable | 0-2 minutes

> Hi [name], Kelly here from Ai With Soul. Thanks for making the time. You mentioned [one thing from their enquiry]. Tell me a little about what's happening with that.

*Let them answer. Then set the purpose.*

> I'll ask a few questions about what you'd like help with, then explain how the workshop would fit and what it costs. We can work out whether it's right for you. Sound good?

## 2. Find the job they want help with | 2-7 minutes

> What made you enquire now?

> Where does [the job they named] get stuck at the moment?

*If they need help choosing:*

> Is it getting your content made and out there, keeping on top of the admin, or something else?

> Talk me through what you do now, from starting that job to finishing it.

> What have you tried to make it easier? What happened?

*If the answer is broad:*

> Can you give me an example from this week?

## 3. Make the outcome specific | 7-12 minutes

> Which part takes the most time or keeps coming back to you?

> When that happens, what gets pushed aside?

> If we made that easier, what would you want your working week to look like?

*If time matters, ask for their estimate. Do not supply an hours-saved figure or turn their estimate into a promised saving.*

> Roughly how much of your week does it take now?

> Is anyone else involved in deciding whether you come along?

*If yes, note who and what they need to know. Do not try to bypass them.*

## 4. Say it back before explaining the offer | 12-14 minutes

> So you want [their desired outcome]. At the moment, [their task] keeps taking [their time or energy], and you've tried [their attempt] but [what got in the way]. Have I got that right?

*Wait for their answer and correct your summary if needed.*

> Of everything you've mentioned, would [one practical job] make the most useful place to start?

## 5. Recommend the workshop | 14-19 minutes

*Use this when their needs fit a day of guided building. Keep your explanation under three minutes.*

> From what you've told me, I'd recommend the one-day workshop in [confirmed city] on [confirmed date]. You said you want to learn how to use the system in your own business, with someone beside you while you get it working.

> The system uses your business information to prepare work such as post copy and email drafts for you to check.

> We get you set up beforehand with a step-by-step installation video. Then you bring your laptop and spend the day working on your business with me. It is a group workshop with up to ten businesses, and I work with each of you. For you, I'd start with [the practical job you agreed], so you can [their desired outcome].

*Choose the relevant example, rather than listing everything the system can do.*

*Content example:*

> We'd work through how to take a video you've filmed and prepare the edit, captions and post copy in your style, ready for you to check.

*Admin example:*

> We'd work through [their agreed task], using your own business information, and show you how to check the result before you use it.

*Continue:*

> The day runs from 9 until 4, with lunch and morning and afternoon tea included. Afterwards, you have 30 days of support in the Skool group, including the Tuesday live builds, so you've got somewhere to ask when you try it back at work.

> You keep what you build in your own accounts. Your software subscriptions are separate, and I'll confirm what you need before you book. After the 30 days, you can choose to move to the paid plan in the same Skool group. That is optional.

> How does that fit with what you were hoping for?

*If their proposed job needs a custom integration or more time, say what you can verify about workshop fit. Do not promise three completed automations by lunch.*

## 6. Say the fee and ask for the decision | 19-23 minutes

> The workshop in [city] on [date], including the day with me and the 30 days of support, is [say the current fee and applicable tax/payment terms].

*Stop. Let them respond. Answer any question before asking:*

> Would you like to go ahead and book your place?

*If they ask the price earlier, answer then:*

> Yes. The workshop fee is [say the current fee and terms]. That includes the day and 30 days of support. Let's check it covers what you need.

## 7. Finish the call clearly | 23-30 minutes

### If yes

> Great. I'll send the secure booking link through now. It is for the [city] workshop on [date] and includes the day and 30 days of support. Have you got it?

*On the phone, use the agreed channel. On Zoom, use chat. State the amount due now under the approved standard payment terms and any remaining payment. Only offer an alternative arrangement if Kelly has already established it for this offer, the same for everyone. Never ask them to read out card details. Do not invent a payment plan.*

> Take a moment to check the details and terms. I'm here if you've got a question while you book.

*Confirm the payment before saying the place is secured.*

> You're booked in. Next you'll get the confirmation and installation instructions. Bring your laptop and a few jobs you'd like help with. We'll start with [their priority].

### If they need something answered

> What's the main thing you'd like to feel clearer about before deciding?

*Use the matching response below. Answer the concern once, then check whether it helped. A clear no ends the sales conversation.*

### If they want a follow-up

> What do you need to check before you can decide?

> Would [a time you've checked] work for a short call once you've had that conversation?

*Book only if they agree. Confirm who will be there and what you will send. If they do not want a follow-up, respect that.*

### If no or the fit is wrong

> Thanks for being clear. I appreciate you taking the time to talk it through.

*If it is a fit issue, explain the specific reason without starting another pitch.*

## When they raise an objection

### “It's a lot of money.”

> I understand. Is it more than you can spend at the moment, or are you unsure you'd get enough value from the day?

*Value concern:*

> You said you wanted [their outcome]. The part of the day that supports that is [the relevant activity], followed by the 30 days of support. What would you need to see or understand to decide whether that's worthwhile for you?

*Budget concern:*

> Thanks for telling me. We can leave it there if the amount doesn't work for you.

*Only discuss a payment option if Kelly has already established it for this offer. Never improvise a discount.*

### “I need to think about it.”

> Of course. What part would you like to think through a little more?

*Listen and answer what they name. Then:*

> Does that clear that part up, or is there something else you need to check?

*If they still want time, use the follow-up ending. Do not repeat the close in a loop.*

### “I need to speak to my partner.”

> Of course. What do you think they'll want to know?

> Would it help to have a short call together so they can ask me those questions?

### “I can't take a whole day out.”

> Is it this particular date that's difficult, or taking a full day away from the business?

*Date issue: only discuss another confirmed date in their own city if they want one; discuss another city only if they ask. Learning-time issue:*

> The workshop does need you there for the day. Do you want to learn how to do this yourself with support, or would you prefer someone to build it for you?

*If they choose done for you, get permission to discuss bespoke and use the separate script. Do not divert someone who wants the workshop.*

### “I'm worried I won't keep up.”

> Which part worries you most: getting set up, following the steps on the day, or remembering it afterwards?

> The installation video covers the setup, I'm with you during the day, and you've got the support group afterwards. Let's check [their specific concern] so you know what to expect.

### “Will this work for my business?”

> Let's take the job you mentioned: [their task]. What would a useful result look like?

*Explain the part you know fits. If you need to check a tool or connection:*

> I need to check [specific dependency] before I promise that part. I'll confirm it before you book.

### “I'll learn it myself.”

> You may prefer that. What have you tried so far, and where would support make a difference, if anywhere?

*If they have a workable plan, respect it. If they name a gap, explain the relevant workshop help.*

### “What happens after the 30 days? Are there other costs?”

> You keep what you've built. You pay for your own software accounts. The included support lasts 30 days. After that, moving to the paid plan in the same Skool group is your choice. I'll go through the current options and costs with you before you commit.

### “I've seen a cheaper workshop.”

> What is included in the one you're comparing it with?

*Listen, then explain the relevant difference without criticising the other provider.*

> This includes the installation preparation, a day working on your own business with me, and 30 days of support afterwards. Which of those would be useful to you?

*If they mention a competitor's guarantee, review your actual booking terms. Do not match a promise or invent one.*

### “What if it doesn't do what I need?”

> What would it need to do for you to feel the day was worthwhile?

*Check that requirement against the offer. If they mean refunds or guarantees:*

> Let's go through the booking terms before you pay so you know where you stand. I can explain the day and support that's included, and I'll tell you if the job you're describing needs something different.

*Read the actual terms. Do not invent a guarantee or imply every custom job will be completed that day.*

### “I'm concerned about confidential information.”

> That's something we should check before using it. What type of information are you working with, and who approves which tools you can use?

> We'll establish what can be used and what needs to stay out. We can start with a non-sensitive example while those permissions are checked.

*Own accounts do not mean information never reaches a software provider. Do not promise local-only processing or blanket compliance.*
